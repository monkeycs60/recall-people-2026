# ASO competitive positioning - Recall People

Date: 2026-05-20

## TL;DR

Claude's current direction is right on emotion, but too narrow when it says "friend". The market is split into three clear territories:

- Name recall: "never forget a name again", names/faces/location/groups.
- Relationship notes: "remember people", notes, reminders, check-ins.
- Professional network memory: "AI contact manager", voice/card capture, semantic search, career/networking payoff.

Recall People should not try to be only a friend-memory app, a contact manager, or a name-reminder app. The clearest position is:

> **Recall People is an AI memory for the people you meet: say who you met, and it turns conversations into names, context, reminders, and follow-up prompts.**

This keeps the emotional benefit, but covers friends, colleagues, clients, family, conference contacts, and casual acquaintances.

Recommended App Store direction:

- App name: `Recall People: AI Memory`
- Subtitle: `Names, notes & follow-ups`
- Main promise: `Never blank on a person again.`
- First screenshot story: pain -> voice capture -> AI extraction -> prepared profile -> timely follow-up.

## What the competitors teach

### 1. The first promise is almost always a shame-relief promise

Competitors rarely start with "AI summaries" or "contact profiles". They start with the uncomfortable human moment:

- You forgot a name.
- You forgot what someone told you.
- You do not know what to ask next.
- You need to walk into a meeting prepared.

Recall People's current first headline, `Never miss what matters in people's lives`, is true but a little abstract. It asks the user to imagine the pain. Stronger App Store copy should name the pain directly.

Best direction:

> `Never blank on a person again.`

It covers names, facts, conversations, and follow-ups without limiting the app to friends.

### 2. "Friend" is emotionally warm but commercially narrow

The friend angle is valuable, but the competitor set shows that buyers think in broader contexts: conferences, school functions, neighbors, families, clients, church, sales, networking, colleagues, and professional referrals.

Use "friend" inside examples, not as the main category.

Better vocabulary:

- `people you meet`
- `people who matter`
- `names, notes, follow-ups`
- `walk in prepared`
- `follow up naturally`
- `your relationship memory`

Avoid making the app sound like either:

- a CRM for sales teams, or
- a sentimental journal only for close friends.

### 3. Voice + AI review is Recall People's clearest differentiation

Many competitors have notes, groups, reminders, or name storage. Fewer make the input feel effortless.

Recall People should make the first three screenshots prove:

1. I forgot / I blank.
2. I speak for 15 seconds.
3. AI turns that into structured memory I control.

The current used screenshots put language selection too early and do not show the AI review/magic moment soon enough.

### 4. Current screenshots look premium but over-explain the product

The visual system is stronger than most competitors: cleaner, more modern, warmer, and less amateur. The issue is not quality. The issue is cognitive load.

Specific problems:

- Screenshot 1 has a good broad promise, but the phone contains many names, tags, dates, and a large floating profile card. It is attractive, but the user must decode it.
- Screenshot 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8 show paragraphs or multiple cards inside the phone. At App Store size this becomes texture, not meaning.
- Screenshot 4, languages, appears before core value. It answers an edge concern before the user understands why they want the app.
- Screenshot 6, upcoming, is a key feature but currently reads like a list. It needs one event and a notification proof.
- Screenshot 7, what to ask next, is a strong differentiator but should show one suggested question, not three.

The fix is not more copy. The fix is more hierarchy:

- One headline.
- One proof object.
- One focused phone state.
- One tiny callout if needed.

## Recommended positioning

### Main category

`AI people memory`

This is broad enough to include personal and professional use, but specific enough to avoid "personal CRM" blandness.

### Main promise

`Never blank on a person again.`

Why this works:

- It is concrete and emotional.
- It includes names without being only about names.
- It includes conversation details and follow-ups.
- It is less friend-only than `Be the friend who remembers`.
- It is less business-only than `AI contact manager`.

### Support promise

`Say who you met. Recall turns it into names, notes, reminders, and follow-ups.`

This explains the mechanism quickly.

### Product enemy

The enemy is not "bad contact management". The enemy is the blank moment:

- blanking on a name,
- forgetting the detail someone shared,
- missing the right follow-up moment,
- walking into a conversation cold.

## ASO copy recommendation

### App name

Recommended:

`Recall People: AI Memory`

Alternatives:

- `Recall People: Voice Memory`
- `Recall People: Relationship AI`

Rationale: `AI Memory` is broad and searchable. `Voice Memory` is more differentiated but less category-clear. `Relationship AI` is tempting, but sounds more abstract and SaaS-like.

### Subtitle

Recommended:

`Names, notes & follow-ups`

Alternatives:

- `Remember everyone you meet`
- `Voice notes to follow-ups`
- `Never blank on people`

Rationale: the current subtitle, `Remember Everyone You Meet`, is good but soft. `Names, notes & follow-ups` tells the App Store scanner exactly what the app does, and adds searchable intent.

### Promotional text

Recommended:

`Just say who you met. Recall turns quick voice notes into people profiles, upcoming reminders, and follow-up prompts.`

### Opening description

Recommended:

```text
Ever blank on someone you just met?

Recall People turns quick voice notes into names, context, reminders, and follow-up prompts, so you show up prepared with friends, colleagues, clients, and anyone who matters.
```

Then explain:

- capture after any conversation,
- AI extracts facts/dates/follow-ups,
- you review/control what is saved,
- Recall reminds you before it matters,
- you can ask your own network questions later.

### Keywords

Recommended 94-character set:

`personal crm,contacts,names,memory,reminders,voice notes,ai,birthdays,networking,relationships`

## Screenshot strategy

### New first-five sequence

The first five screenshots should be treated as the conversion story. Everything after is secondary.

| # | Headline | Sub-line | Source screen | Visual rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Never blank on a person again` | `names, details, follow-ups` | Contacts list | One visible contact card, not a wall of tags |
| 2 | `Just say who you met` | `15-second voice notes` | Voice capture | Big microphone, minimal form text |
| 3 | `AI finds what matters` | `facts, dates, next steps` | AI review | Show extraction checklist, not language picker |
| 4 | `Walk in prepared` | `what they told you, at a glance` | Contact profile | Crop to summary + next action only |
| 5 | `Follow up at the right time` | `before birthdays, meetings, trips` | Upcoming / notification | One event plus iOS-style notification overlay |

### Secondary screenshots

| # | Headline | Sub-line | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | `Know what to ask next` | `natural prompts from your notes` | Differentiates from simple reminders |
| 7 | `Ask your network anything` | `find people by context` | Differentiates from name-reminder apps |
| 8 | `Works in 5 languages` | `EN, FR, ES, DE, IT` | Trust/localization, but not core conversion |

### What to remove or delay

- Do not show the language picker before the core product story.
- Do not lead with `friend`; use examples inside the phone.
- Do not use screenshots where the phone contains 3+ paragraphs or 3+ cards.
- Do not make "notes" a headline before the user understands the capture-to-follow-up loop.

### Visual production rules

- Max 6-7 words in headline.
- Max 4-6 words in sub-line, or remove sub-line entirely.
- The phone should show one readable object: one note, one reminder, one extracted result, one profile summary.
- Replace dense in-phone content with one external callout if needed.
- Use stronger background rhythm than the current all-lavender set: light, brand purple, warm light, dark/premium, light.
- Keep the product UI as the hero. Mascots/characters can be accents, but should not compete with the phone in the first five screenshots.

## Product tweaks worth considering for screenshots

These are small product/detail changes that would improve App Store clarity:

- Add or seed a "Meeting prep" / "At a glance" contact state with only 3 bullets and one next action.
- Add a screenshot seed where Upcoming has exactly one high-value event, not a list.
- Add a screenshot seed where follow-up prompts show one large recommended question.
- Make AI review visually central: extracted facts, dates, reminders, and follow-ups should be visible before saving.
- In Assistant answers, show a short answer plus a visible source chip. Avoid long paragraphs in App Store screenshots.

## Approach options

### Option A - Broad relationship memory

Positioning: `Remember everyone you meet.`

Pros:

- Broad and inclusive.
- Works for friends, family, colleagues, clients, and events.
- Keeps warmth.

Cons:

- Slightly generic; competitors already say similar things.

### Option B - Utility painkiller

Positioning: `Never blank on a person again.`

Pros:

- Immediate pain.
- Clearer than "relationship intelligence".
- Covers names, context, and follow-ups.
- Strongest first screenshot hook.

Cons:

- Slightly less elegant than the current emotional copy.

### Option C - Professional memory copilot

Positioning: `Walk into every conversation prepared.`

Pros:

- Strong paid-user angle.
- Differentiates from casual name apps.
- Good for networking, founders, sales, consultants.

Cons:

- Risks sounding business-only and moving into RecallVerse territory.

## Recommendation

Use Option B as the hook, with Option A as the category and Option C as a later proof point.

In practice:

- Store listing headline: `Recall People: AI Memory`
- Subtitle: `Names, notes & follow-ups`
- First screenshot: `Never blank on a person again`
- Second screenshot: `Just say who you met`
- Third screenshot: `AI finds what matters`
- Fourth screenshot: `Walk in prepared`
- Fifth screenshot: `Follow up at the right time`

This gives Recall People a sharper promise than Revere, more emotional breadth than RecallVerse, more sophistication than the name-reminder apps, and a clearer conversion path than the current friend-centric direction.
